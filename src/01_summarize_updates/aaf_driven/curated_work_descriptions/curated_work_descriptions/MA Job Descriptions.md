material for distribution to the public. This information is widely used

project managers to build and run their probability-based sample

proposed action. The unintentional release of confidential information
can cause significant loss of good will as well as jeopardizing
respondent relations.</p>
<p>Using quality assurance and mathematical quality control methods,
monitors the outgoing quality of work performed by operational or
processing staff doing data capture, coding, editing and other
processing operations to ensure that the average outgoing quality level
meets agreed-upon levels for the process as a whole and for individual
operators. Non-compliance could lead to data whose quality is not
adequate for the intended use of the data.</p>

developmental work on new methods of survey methodology.</p>

<p>Techniques for maintaining good respondent relations and for

<td><p><strong>Responsibility</strong></p>
<p><strong>Information for the Use of Others</strong></p>
<p>Researches and recommends the methodological and statistical
frameworks for data gathering processes for census, survey or
administrative data sources to meet the statistical information program
needs of clients.</p>
<p>Explains the methodologies, their potential, limitations and
applicability in the development and integration of surveys or phases of
surveys for clients as input to their analytical requirements.</p>
<p>Research on new methods and techniques of data collection, processing
and analysis to identify potential benefits in terms of cost, timeliness
and quality for existing or new surveys<em>.</em></p>
<p>Provides expert advice on new statistical methods to clients involved
in providing statistical information as specified in legislation for
service to the people of Canada.</p>
<p>Proposes revisions to statistical methods and techniques based on
research to clients to enhance or improve existing statistical
information products.</p>
<p>Prepares comprehensive research reports detailing proposed procedures
and methodologies for steering committees, external advisory committees
and other departments to be used to assess survey programs and to make
recommendations concerning redesign or new development to those surveys
that will impact departmental programs.</p>
<p>Adds to the pool of knowledge within the department through
exchanging technical information, survey experiences and results of
research with professionals in other departments, statistical agencies
and universities of Canada and foreign countries.</p>
<p>Researches, prepares and writes technical reports and papers for
publication or presentation at conferences or seminars, and course
material in survey methodology for departmental and outside clients to
promote the use of good statistical methods.</p>
<p>Recommending topics and invitees for symposia and workshops on
statistics and survey methodology for use by the organizing
committee<em>.</em></p>
<p>As an expert consultant on new methods, provides instruction and
guidance to researchers in the field about new statistical technology,
state of the art research and new experimental methods to improve the
level of science.</p>
<p>Provides peer reviews of research papers and review articles for
scientific journals to assist the journal editors in deciding which are
suitable for publication.</p>
<p>Creates or adds to the body of scientific statistical knowledge for
use by practitioners in improving the efficiency, effectiveness and
reliability of statistical information systems.</p>
<p>Proposes and recommends the skill levels and resources required to
carry out the section’s research responsibilities.</p>
<p><strong>Money</strong></p>

<p>Plans and develops a budget for the section in accordance with
departmental policy and procedures. This responsibility includes
determining the allocation of methodological and operational input to
research projects in progress and also for proposed projects submitted
as part of the Long Term Planning Process.</p>
<p>Tracks time spent on projects vis-a-vis progress on deliverables
compared to consumption.</p>
<p>Money flow:</p>
<p>Manages time spent for cost recovery activities for the purpose of
recovering such costs from the client.</p>
<p>Spending Funds:</p>
<p>Negotiates and arranges contracts with consulting firms, universities
and professional statisticians for the acquisition of professional
statistical services or research fellowships.</p>
<p>Recommends expenditures for the acquisition of statistical books,
reference materials or software and printing to carry out methodology
projects; training and attendance by staff at conferences for
professional development.</p>
<p>While in travel status, spends funds according to limits set out in
the Travel Directives.</p>
<p><strong>Physical Assets and Products</strong></p>
<p>Ensures the protection of confidential information, contained in
working files and paper copies, acquired in the research process and
takes responsibility for their proper disposal or destruction after use.
These active items are used, by own and other staff, in the production
of information products for use by internal and external clients.</p>
<p>Organizes, on a rotating basis, international symposia and
workshops<em> </em>related to new statistical and survey methodology for
the exchange of information on current experience and research
developments amongst invitees and others interested in statistics and
survey methodology<em>.</em></p>
<p><strong>Ensuring Compliance</strong></p>
<p>Evaluates and ensures application of procedures and practices
developed by staff for use by survey statisticians, technicians and
processing staff to abide by disclosure provisions or prevent breach of
confidentiality, of data cells or data sets, as stipulated in the
Statistics Act. Advises staff on acceptability of proposed actions. The
unintentional release of confidential information can cause significant
loss of good will as well as jeopardizing respondent relations.</p>
<p>Points out to clients the requirements of Statistics Canada’s
policies relevant to statistical methodology such as The Review Of
Information Products, Record Linkage or standards and guidelines for
non-response.</p>
<p><strong>Skills</strong></p>
<p><strong>Job Content Knowledge</strong></p>
<p><strong>The work requires knowledge of and research skill in the
theory, principles and application of mathematics, statistics and survey
methodology including recent developments, described in current
literature and through contacts with other methodologists inside and
outside the department, as they apply to:</strong></p>
<p>Survey sampling methods including: use and maintenance of sampling
frames, stratification, sample size determination and allocation, sample
selection, multistage sampling, multiphase sampling, area sampling,
sample rotation, weighting and estimation, variance estimation, use of
modelling, and techniques to measure and assess sampling and
non-sampling errors in surveys. This knowledge is required to meet the
section’s responsibility to provide methodology research advice and
service (including advancing the science and developing new methods for
the Canadian statistical system) to departmental managers of statistical
programs and to sponsoring clients, with specific information needs, in
other government departments and the private sector.</p>
<p>Data collection principles and methods to initiate and promote the
design of sampling schemes, design of pilot surveys, cognitive testing
of questionnaires and manuals and design of components of data
collection systems.</p>
<p>Probability theory and mathematical statistics to lead a section to
design sampling frames and sample schemes, to develop methods for
imputation, to develop and apply methods of record linkage, and to
develop new methods of survey methodology.</p>
<p>Edit and imputation methods and automated coding systems for
application in surveys and components of survey support infrastructure
methods and automated coding methods for surveys.</p>
<p>Techniques for maintaining good respondent relations and for
minimising respondent burden in the design of sampling schemes,
interview procedures and in developing new methods of survey
methodology.</p>
<p>Methods for the analysis of data from surveys with complex designs
(e.g. involving stratification, multistage sampling, multiphase
sampling, etc.)</p>
<p>Quality assurance and quality control methods, record linkage
methods, time series methods, and disclosure avoidance techniques to
ensure that methodology is developed that will assure the accuracy and
confidentiality needs of the survey sponsor and to facilitate
analysis.</p>
<p>Operations research, multivariate statistical methods, design of
experiments, analysis methods for categorical and continuous data to
advance the design and conduct of analytical and evaluation studies (of
data from complex surveys, both cross-sectional and longitudinal) and of
pilot surveys.</p>
<p>Recent developments in statistics and survey methodology, as
described in current literature and through contacts with other
methodologists inside and outside the department, to conduct
developmental work to benefit surveys.</p>
<p>Skill is required in the application of research approaches and
processes, evaluation and effectiveness measurement, problem and issue
identification, scientific information/data analysis and interpretation
to develop new methods of statistical methodology.</p>
<p>Knowledge of the use of office automation software including word
processors, spreadsheets, presentation graphics, email, databases,
intranet and internet to prepare technical notes and documentation.</p>
<p>Knowledge of information technology to understand its application to
statistical methods used in survey taking and to further development of
survey methodology.</p>
<p>Management and co-ordination skills and knowledge of general and
project management methods, techniques and practices to manage and
co-ordinate the activities of a methodology section or research team.
This involves establishing work priorities, estimating resource
requirements, allocating resources, evaluating risks versus benefits and
developing contingency plans to resolve problems. At this level, a
number of recurring and special projects are undertaken and managed
concurrently.</p>
<p>Knowledge of the application of problem solving techniques and skill
in advising clients and in determining, researching and assessing
statistical and survey methods.</p>
<p>Skill in resolving trade-offs between cost, timeliness, quality and
response burden for survey taking activities. This skill is needed to
identify components in which new methods and techniques may have maximal
impact and so to allocate resources.</p>
<p>Skill in the management of knowledge-based staff in co-ordinating the
professional work of mathematical statisticians engaged in statistical
studies.</p>
<p>Skill in the techniques of effective supervision including
delegation, interpersonal conflict resolution and performance appraisal
to lead staff effectively.</p>
<p>Skill in co-ordinating and assigning staff to projects to
concurrently meet staff needs for professional development as well as to
meet the competing priorities of multiple projects.</p>
