## Statistics Canada Mandate and Organizational Structure

Statistics Canada provides statistical information and analysis about all aspects of Canadian life in order to develop and ensure public policies based on valid and reliable data. As a member of the [Innovation, Science and Economic Development portfolio](http://www.ic.gc.ca/eic/site/icgc.nsf/eng/h_00022.html) (link accessible on Network B only), Statistics Canada produces statistics that help Canadians better understand their country—its population, resources, economy, society and culture.

Under the [*Statistics Act*](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/s-19/fulltext.html) (Net B), Statistics Canada’s mandate is to “collect, compile, analyse, abstract and publish statistical information relating to the commercial, industrial, financial, social, economic and general activities and condition of the people” of Canada. Statistics Canada has the following two main objectives:

1. Provide statistical information and analysis about Canada’s economic and social structure to
   * develop and evaluate public policies and programs
   * improve public and private decision making for the benefit of all Canadians.
2. Promote sound statistical standards and practices by
   * reducing the burden on respondents through greater use of data-sharing agreements (e.g., annual tax records, monthly employee payroll records and customs records)
   * using common concepts and classifications to provide better-quality data
   * working with the provinces and territories to achieve greater efficiency in data collection and to avoid duplication
   * improving statistical methods and systems through joint research studies and projects.

Statistics Canada, led by the [Chief Statistician](http://icn-rci.statcan.ca/999/999_000-eng.html) (Net A), is organized into five different fields, with each field being responsible for different portfolios (see [organizational chart](http://icn-rci.statcan.ca/07/pdf/orgchrt-eng.pdf); Net A). The agency itself consists of a head office, and multiple regional offices and research data centres. There are eight regional offices, located within three regions (Western, Central, and Eastern). Each region is responsible for about one-third of the total population of the country.

The responsibilities and activities of each region are identical. Each region is accountable for the following:

1. The **Operations team** is responsible for managing and collecting survey information from respondents using various collection techniques such as electronic questionnaires, telephone interviews, and personal interviews.
2. The **Statistical Information Service team** is responsible for providing data, information, and education services to the public.

Please take the time to review the [New Employee Handbook](http://icn-rci.statcan.ca/24/24g/24g_000-eng.html) (Net A) for more information on Statistics Canada and some useful tools for new employees.

## CCJCSS Background and Mandate

The Canadian Centre for Justice and Community Safety Statistics (CCJCSS) was created in 1981 as a division of Statistics Canada. The CCJCSS is the operational arm of a federal-provincial-territorial (FPT) partnership, known as the National Justice Statistics Initiative (NJSI). The mandate of the NJSI is to provide information to the justice community and the public on the nature and extent of crime and victimization and the administration of criminal and civil justice in Canada. The CCJCSS is responsible for the development, collection, integration and analysis of data and the development of national- and jurisdictional-level indicators.

In addition to the Director’s Office, the CCJCSS is divided into seven program areas/units: the Policing Services Program, the Courts Program, the Corrections Program, the Analysis Program, the Data Access and Dissemination Unit, the Data Development Unit, and Strategic Partners and FPT Relations. ### Policing Services Program

*Chief: Julie Sauvé*

The Policing Services Program develops, collects, integrates, analyzes, and disseminates information through multiple surveys. The [Uniform Crime Reporting Survey (UCR)](https://www23.statcan.gc.ca/imdb/p2SV.pl?Function=getSurvey&SDDS=3302) measures police-reported crime across Canada and is completed by extracting data from record management systems in order to report on trends in criminal incidents reported to the police. The [Homicide Survey](https://www23.statcan.gc.ca/imdb/p2SV.pl?Function=getSurvey&SDDS=3315) provides detailed data on all reported homicides in Canada, including characteristics of the incidents, victims, and accused persons. Finally, the [Police Administration Survey (PAS)](https://www23.statcan.gc.ca/imdb/p2SV.pl?Function=getSurvey&SDDS=3301) collects data on Canadian police personnel to measure the ratio of officers to population, operating expenditures and anticipated staffing needs. Each year, the team is responsible for the annual production of standard data tables and other analytical products released to the public involving these surveys. The Policing Services Program also works collaboratively with other bodies of the National Justice Statistics Initiative, including the Liaison Officers Committee (LOC) and the Canadian Association of Chiefs of Police (CACP).

### Courts Program

*Chief: Joel Roy*

The mandate of the Courts Program is to collect, process, analyze, and disseminate information related to the operation of Canada’s criminal and civil courts and related programs. The Courts Program currently maintains three large microdata surveys and administers several other analytical projects. The [Integrated Criminal Courts Survey (ICCS)](https://www23.statcan.gc.ca/imdb/p2SV.pl?Function=getSurvey&SDDS=3312) is an administrative survey that collects microdata from jurisdictional courts’ information systems on pending and completed charges, appearances, and cases for federal statute offences heard in youth courts and adult provincial and superior criminal courts. The primary objective of the [Civil Court Survey (CCS)](https://www23.statcan.gc.ca/imdb/p2SV.pl?Function=getSurvey&SDDS=5052) is to develop and maintain a national civil court database of statistical information on court events and cases. It is intended to collect comparable, national level baseline data on civil court activity in Canada. Finally, the [Survey of Maintenance Enforcement Programs (SMEP)](https://www23.statcan.gc.ca/imdb/p2SV.pl?Function=getSurvey&SDDS=3324) collects information on child and spousal support payments and cases from provincial and territorial maintenance enforcement programs (MEPs). These programs exist in each province and territory to assist in the collection and enforcement of child and spousal support payments.

### Corrections Program

*Chief: Paul Robinson*

The Corrections Program collects, processes, analyzes, and disseminates information related to the operation of Canada’s correctional services through multiple surveys, including the [Canadian Correctional Services Survey (CCSS)](https://www23.statcan.gc.ca/imdb/p2SV.pl?Function=getSurvey&SDDS=5251), the Integrated Correctional Services Survey (ICSS), [Adult Correctional Services survey (ACS)](https://www23.statcan.gc.ca/imdb/p2SV.pl?Function=getSurvey&SDDS=3306), [Youth Custody and Community Services Survey (YCCS)](https://www23.statcan.gc.ca/imdb/p2SV.pl?Function=getSurvey&SDDS=3323), and [Corrections Key Indicator Report for Adults and Youth (CKIR)](https://www23.statcan.gc.ca/imdb/p2SV.pl?Function=getSurvey&SDDS=3313).

The key measures produced and disseminated by the Corrections Program are admissions and average counts. Admissions data are collected through the ACS and YCCS surveys and produced with ICSS and CCSS microdata. Admissions are counted each time a person begins any period of supervision in a correctional institution or in the community. Admissions data provide workload information, representing the number of entries of persons, during a fiscal year, to remand, sentenced custody or a community supervision program, regardless of the previous legal status.

Average counts data are collected through the KIR for adults and youth and produced with CCSS microdata. Average count data provide population information—the number of offenders in custody or community on an average day or at the end of an average month. Average counts are used to produce incarceration rates and information on the average daily cost per inmate.

New CCSS indicators on intakes to corrections, as well as overrepresentation within the correctional system, are currently under development.

### Analysis Program

*Chief: Kathy AuCoin/Martin Turcotte*

The Analysis Unit is responsible for the production and dissemination of analytical reports on topics such as trends in self-reported victimization, the nature and prevalence of police-reported crime, and sentencing outcomes. The program is also the Statistics Canada lead for research on family violence (including the federal Family Violence Initiative and all FPT and interdepartmental activities), gender-based violence, victim matrices, and analysis of the General Social Survey on Victimization. The unit is also responsible for working with other StatCan divisions in developing new content to capture information on victimization, gender-based violence, child maltreatment, and access to justice indicators.

The Analysis Program produces analytical reports on the following key surveys: the [General Social Survey (GSS) – Victimization/Canadians’ Safety](https://www23.statcan.gc.ca/imdb/p2SV.pl?Function=getSurvey&SDDS=4504), the [Survey of Safety in Public and Private Spaces (SSPPS)](https://www23.statcan.gc.ca/imdb/p2SV.pl?Function=getSurvey&SDDS=5256), the [Survey on Sexual Misconduct at Work (SSMW)](https://www23.statcan.gc.ca/imdb/p2SV.pl?Function=getSurvey&SDDS=5290), and the [Survey of Residential Facilities for Victims of Abuse (SRFVA)](https://www23.statcan.gc.ca/imdb/p2SV.pl?Function=getSurvey&SDDS=5290) and the Canadian Armed Forces Survey on Sexual Miconduct. Some of the team’s recently published reports covered topics such as intimate partner violence, trafficking in persons, and public perceptions of the police.

### Data Access and Dissemination Unit

*Chief: Brianna Jaffray*

The Data Access and Dissemination (DAD) unit provides critical support to all other program areas in the CCJCSS with a primary goal of increasing access to CCJCSS data products (data files and custom tabulations). The Client Services Team within DAD responds to, and/or coordinates, all incoming requests for CCJCSS (e.g., special data requests, inquiries about officially released products). These requests can come from the general public, students, academia, other governments departments, police services, the media, and more. DAD is also responsible for the coordination and dissemination of official releases (e.g., Juristats). DAD also provides critical technical support to other program areas (e.g., data verification, writing advanced SAS programming for various needs) and supports other teams with the process that must be undertaken to make CCJCSS data publicly available through Statistics Canada’s Research Data Centre’s (RDCs) and other data access mechanisms.

### Data Development Unit

*Chief:* *Cameron McIntosh*

The Data Development Unit (DDU) works on special projects across a range of justice and community safety-related topics. Typically, the projects undertaken in the DDU involve addressing data gaps that have been identified by CCJCSS as well as our partners, stakeholders, and clients. These can include projects that examine the feasibility of collecting new data on a particular justice topic as well as projects that analyze existing data sources in new ways (e.g., linking justice data across multiple sectors to examine re-contact in the justice system). DDU activities tend to respond to emerging data and information needs of clients external to Statistics Canada (e.g., other federal/provincial government organizations). The DDU works closely with other CCJCSS program areas in order to successfully undertake these projects.

### Community Safety Program

*Chief:* *Kimberley Boyuk*

In May 2019. the Centre expanded it’s mandate to include aspects of community safety related to non-criminal events in order to formally recognize work being done on such areas. A Community Safety Program was established to address emerging and evolving data needs in areas such as structural fires, emergency response to natural disasters and other critical events, other first responder data, and missing persons.

The work of this program is mainly cost-recovery and involves data development in non-criminal community safety issues.

### Strategic Partnerships and FPT Relations

*Chief:* *Luc Akakpo*

This unit is a new addition to the CCJCSS in 2021-22 and is intended to strengthen CCJCSS capacity to participate in Federal, Provincial and Territorial (FPT) tables, including the Liaison Officers Committee, which has been recognized by FPT Deputy Ministers Responsible for Justice and Public Safety as a main table. This unit is also responsible for secretariat functions for FPT operational advisory committees co-chaired by the CCJCSS, and LOC. In addition, the unit participates in federal interdepartmental working groups on horizontal initiatives in the justice and community safety domains, with a view of identifying data needs to inform the policy process, and working towards addressing those needs. The unit also leads internal corporate reporting, strategic planning and other internal initiatives.

### Other Resources

Depending on the nature of your work at CCJCSS, you may need to familiarize yourself with some of the processes and tools listed below. **Please note:** all hyperlinks in this section are only accessible on **Network A**.

* [Confluence](https://confluence.statcan.ca/): Confluence is a wiki software tool that is used by teams at Statistics Canada to share information and collaborate. It can be accessed on Network A using your username and password. For example, the [Social Data Linkage Environment (SDLE) team’s Confluence page](https://confluence.statcan.ca/pages/viewpage.action?pageId=63540611) provides a detailed overview of the record linkage process. The SDLE facilitates data integration across multiple domains (e.g., health, justice, education, income) using existing administrative and survey data.
* [Jira](https://jira.statcan.ca/secure/Dashboard.jspa): Jira is a project management software used by Statistics Canada. It can be accessed on Network A using your username and password. This [Quick Guide](https://confluence.statcan.ca/display/ASSORT/JIRA%2BQuick%2BGuide) provides an overview of how to use Jira. It is primarily used to submit requests to service areas across Statistics Canada (e.g., Operations and Integration Division, Collection Planning and Research Division, Data Governance Support). If you’re working on a project that falls under the Departmental Project Management Framework (see below), you will also be using Jira to track and report on project milestones, issues, etc.
* [Acquiring alternative data](http://icn-rci.statcan.ca/35/35c/35c_087-eng.html): Alternative data are defined as any data that are not collected through a Statistics Canada survey. Statistics Canada has established a mandatory governance process to acquire or integrate alternative data into a statistical program. Managed by the Data Governance Support (DGS) team in the Office of Privacy Management and Information Coordination (OPMIC) Division, the process ensures compliance with relevant governing instruments while supporting statistical program managers, data custodians and data stewards. The general process for acquiring new data is outlined in [this placemat](http://www153.statcan.ca/pdf/w153_r000_01-eng.pdf). DGS is available to support employees throughout the entire data acquisition process. For more information on the process to acquire data, please visit the [DGS Portal](http://www153.statcan.ca/w153_r000-eng.html). For general inquiries, contact the DGS mailbox.
* [Departmental Project Management Framework (DPMF)](http://icn-rci.statcan.ca/35/35c/35c_043-eng.html): the DPMF is a set of standard project management processes, templates and tools. You can use the DPMF to ensure your project’s success throughout all six project stages. Although use of the DPMF is strongly recommended as a project management tool for all projects, it is currently mandatory for projects valued at $500,000 or higher. For questions about the DPMF, please contact the Departmental Project Management Office’s (DPMO) mailbox.
* [Client Relationship Management System (CRMS)](http://stcapp-p07.statcan.ca:8000/psp/CRM91PRD/?cmd=login&languageCd=ENG&): many projects undertaken at the CCJCSS are completed on a [cost recovery basis for external clients](http://icn-rci.statcan.ca/35/35c/35c_032-eng.html). When you enter into an agreement with an external client, you need to formalize it. The CRMS is the corporate tool used to manage all cost-recovery projects at Statistics Canada. It generates letters of agreement (LOAs) and is used by project managers to initiate the invoicing process throughout the duration of the project.

## Mandatory Training

As a new employee, you must complete the suite of [mandatory training courses](http://icn-rci.statcan.ca/07/07m/07m08/07m08_006-eng.html) (Net A).

If you are new to the federal public service generally, or if you have not yet completed these courses in your former department, you must complete (Net B): [List of mandatory training for Statistics Canada employees (statcan.ca)](http://icn-rci.statcan.ca/07/07m/07m08/07m08_007-eng.html).

The above four courses are available through the Canada School of Public Service (CSPS). To participate in a CSPS course, take these steps:

1. Talk to your supervisor about the course you want to take, and when you want to take it, and request their approval.
2. Add the course to your Learning and Development Plan (Section D of the Performance Agreement).
3. Register for the course directly on the CSPS website through [GCcampus](https://idp.csps-efpc.gc.ca/idp/Authn/UserPassword) (Net B). You will need to [create a Gccampus account](https://idp.csps-efpc.gc.ca/idp/RegistrationUtilities) if you do not already have one.

If you are new to Statistics Canada, you must complete the following courses (Net A):

1. [KLICK - Statistics Canada Orientation](http://klick/e/courses/list_courses.asp?category=Mandatory)
2. [KLICK - Confidentiality - New Employees](http://klick/e/courses/list_courses.asp?category=Mandatory)
3. [KLICK - The Mobile Employee](http://klick/e/courses/list_courses.asp?category=Mandatory)

If you are joining CCJCSS in a supervisory role or above (Team Lead, Unit Head, Chief, etc.), please consult [the mandatory training ICN page](http://icn-rci.statcan.ca/07/07m/07m08/07m08_007-eng.html) (Net A) for the additional mandatory training courses at your level.

## Extracurriculars and Professional Development

Despite our new virtual environment, there are plenty of opportunities for you to get involved and get to know your colleagues within CCJCCS, other divisions of Statistics Canada, and other departments or agencies in the federal government. Some of these include:

* **CCJCSS Social Committee:** the CCJCSS has its very own Social Committee that is always looking for new volunteers to help plan and organize virtual social events. If interested, please contact the CCJCSS Social Committee mailbox.
* **Health Justice Official Languages Committee (HJOLC):** CCJCSS has partnered with our Field 8 colleagues in the Health to establish the HJOLC. For resources (e.g., links to training courses through the CSPS) and activities, please visit the [HJOLC’s Confluence Page](https://confluence.statcan.ca/pages/viewpage.action?pageId=492407656) (Net A). Our CCJCSS representative for the committee is currently Adriana Ciccone. If you are interested in joining the Committee or have any OL-related questions, please contact Adriana directly or the HJOLC mailbox.
* **StatCan Professionals Network (SPN):** The [StatCan Professionals Network (SPN)](http://icn-rci.statcan.ca/12/12J/12j_043-eng.html) (Net A) is an inclusive, open, volunteer-run, group of professional StatCan employees who organize professional and social events. Their vision and mission focus on the importance of cultivating unity between all employees and creating an open and positive space for StatCan employees to network. Membership is free and comes with great perks, including access to skill-building workshops, networking opportunities, special project development, and of course, social events. Simply email the network to join!
* **GCconnex:** [GCconnex](https://gcconnex.gc.ca/splash/) (Net B) is a professional networking and collaborative workplace for all federal public servants. It allows them to connect and share information, leveraging the power of networking towards a more effective and efficient public service. [Register for GCconnex](https://gcconnex.gc.ca/register) (Net B) if you would like to connect to other public servants across the Government of Canada.
* **Data Science Network for the Federal Public Service (DSNFPS):** [DSNFPS](https://www.statcan.gc.ca/eng/data-science/network?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=statcan-data-science-20-21) (Net B) is a new data science community that will facilitate data science capacity building and sharing of data science methods across the Government of Canada, allowing the public service as a whole to benefit from the use of automation and new data sources. The Network users share information on training, best practices, cutting-edge projects and more. Statistics Canada, as the country's national statistical agency and trusted source for information, is pleased to fulfill a stewardship role for this network. One their website, you can [subscribe to their newsletter](https://www.statcan.gc.ca/eng/data-science/network/newsletter) to receive regular updates and invitations to important events and resources. Feel free to contact the DSNFPS general mailbox if you have questions or would like more information.
